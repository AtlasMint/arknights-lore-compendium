# v3.1: Sourced Summaries

**Theme:** every summary sentence knows which story told it
**Outcome:** a node's summary holds only what the stories you have ticked have told you, and grows as you tick more.
**Status:** built 2026-10-07. Every task is done; the Doctor's page turned out to have story sections after all, and the lead is the page's non-story sections rather than its first section only. See [Outcome notes](#outcome-notes).

## Goal

[v3](v3-richer-lore.md) hides a summary sentence when it names a term from a story you haven't read. That catches names, not facts. Amiya's summary says she "contracted Oripathy and was rescued by the Doctor": Amiya, Oripathy and the Doctor are all visible from the Prologue, so the sentence shows from the start, yet the rescue is a reveal from Babel, released in 2024. The wiki knows this. The sentence ends in a citation to stage BB-4, and the stage page names its story.

This version gives each sentence a story, from the wiki's own structure, and gates the sentence on that story being ticked. It is the other half of what v3 borrowed from Final Fantasy XVI's lore design: an entry that lengthens as the story progresses, rather than one with sentences struck out.

## Why not the page history

The idea considered first was to show each page as it stood at the real-world date matching the player's progress. The wiki's history goes back to May 2019 and any revision can be fetched by date, but the lore wasn't there to fetch:

| Page | First revision | As of December 2020 | Today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amiya | May 2019 | a stats page, no lore prose | |
| Amiya/Story | February 2022 | did not exist | 59 KB, 100 citations |
| Talulah | January 2021 | did not exist | 31 KB |
| Theresa | April 2024 | did not exist | 16 KB |
| Reunion | April 2020 | 7.7 KB | 28 KB |

A player at Episode 06 would get empty summaries for nearly everyone. And the wiki is written from the CN server, about six months ahead of Global, so a revision dated to Global progress already carries the next stories' reveals. When a fact was written down says nothing about which story told it.

In-universe dates are ruled out for the same reason from the other side: the story reveals the past out of order. Babel is set in 1094 and released in 2024; gating by timeline year would show its reveals to someone at Episode 02, since those are the "oldest" events. Release order is the only safe order, and the ticks already give it.

## Where a sentence's story comes from

Three sources on the wiki, used in this order. Each was checked on the pages named under [Measured](#measured-before-building).

### 1. Story-headed sections

A character page's body is one subsection per story: Amiya/Story's **Plot** has Babel, Prologue, Episode 01 to Episode 16 and every side story she appears in, each under its own heading; Talulah's and Theresa's **Story** sections are the same. Every sentence under "Episode 08" belongs to Episode 08 by construction. These are exactly the pages whose lead is lightly cited, because their detail lives here.

Headings that are not a story are skipped: operator records ("Blaze: Will You?"), Integrated Strategies preludes, Act names used as grouping headings on Wei Yenwu's page. Of 190 subsection headings over 20 character pages, 101 name a story in the timeline outright, and most of the rest are operator records.

The matched headings are also the character's list of appearances, which fixes a gap: Amiya's infobox says `appearance = See Plot`, so today's appearance parsing finds nothing for her.

### 2. Citations

Lore pages cite the stage that establishes each claim: `[[BB-4/Story#After operation|BB-4 After]]`, `[[12-16/Story|12-16]]`, `[[M8-8/Story#After operation|M8-8 After]]`. Over nine lore pages, 201 of 268 citations are stage citations; the rest are books, artbooks, operator records and a couple of forum links, which carry no story.

A stage page's `Operation info` template names its story: `episode = 07` for the Main Theme, `intermezzo = Babel (event)`, `sidestory = Near Light`. Episode 08's stages carry codes like `M8-8` and `R8-1`, so the code is never parsed; the stage page is.

A sentence is covered by the citation that closes its run: the first `<ref>` at or after its end within its paragraph, the usual wiki convention of the reference following the claims it supports. A paragraph with no citation, or a sentence after the last one, has no story from this source. `<ref name="x"/>` reuses resolve by name.

### 3. The operator archive files

`Amiya/File` holds the in-game Profile, Clinical Analysis and Archive Files as `{{Archive|title=...|text=...}}` templates. They are what the game shows any player who owns the operator, written at the operator's release and never updated with later story. Amiya's Profile is two sentences, "The public face of Rhodes Island and its top executive…", and makes a better opening than the wiki's lead. The crawl already fetches `/File` for an operator's links, so this costs nothing.

### Not used

- **The story scripts** at every stage's `/Story` page are the ground truth for "has this been told yet", but using them means asking the local model whether a ticked script establishes a sentence. Slow, and the weakest link in the chain. Kept in reserve for uncited lead sentences if the three sources above leave too much ungated.
- **Page history by date** and **in-universe years**, for the reasons above.

## Out of scope

- Verifying facts against the story scripts with the model
- Operator records and Integrated Strategies as stories; their headings are skipped, not gated
- Other servers; the tag is the story, so v4's per-server work carries over unchanged

## Risks

- **A citation covers the wrong sentence.** A sentence between two citations is sometimes covered by the one before it. The check by hand on four pages says how often; if it is common, cover a sentence only when the citation sits in the same sentence or the next.
- **Uncited leads stay ungated.** Kal'tsit's page has one citation; the operator `/Story` leads have few. Those sentences keep today's term rule, which is no worse than now, and their story sections carry the detail.
- **Stage pages on a cold cache.** Nine lore pages cited 124 distinct stages. A 150-node graph could want a few hundred stage pages on first build, at one request a second. Only the lead's citations are resolved, in one batch, and the cache keeps them a week; if the first build is still too slow, resolve stages lazily when a node is selected.
- **Headings drift.** "Grani and the Knight's Treasure" on Nearl's page is not "Grani and the Knights' Treasure". An unmatched heading is skipped, so drift loses a sentence rather than leaking one.

## Outcome notes

### Where the build departs from the plan

- **The lead is wider than "the first section".** Character pages open with a sentence or two and put the substance under a Background heading, so the lead is every sentence outside a story's section, in page order, capped at four. Talulah reads as she did in v3, with stories attached.
- **The Doctor gained sentences.** The plan expected the Doctor's page to read exactly as in v3, having no Story section. It has story headings at a deeper level, so the Doctor now gets one sentence per story too, the same way as everyone else. The lead is unchanged, as the plan required.
- **Stage pages are read per prefix, not per code.** BB-4 and BB-7 belong to the same story, so one page per prefix is fetched and the answer kept for the process. Building a 150-node map around Amiya, Talulah, Theresa and the Doctor took 7 s on a warm page cache, stage pages included.
- **Unreleased stories keep their name.** A sentence cited to a stage of a story not yet on Global carries that story rather than nothing, so it stays hidden until tick-all like any untagged term, and will show by itself once the story is released and ticked.
- **Grouped in the panel.** Sentences from no story in particular come first as one paragraph; the rest are grouped by story, each group ending in "learned in" that story.

### Still owed

- A hand-read of more pages. Four is enough to show the rule works, not how often a citation covers the wrong sentence.
- Headings that drift from story names lose their sentence silently. A count of unmatched headings per build would show whether that matters.
- **Connection labels can still tell a later story.** The label on the Amiya–Doctor link reads "Amiya was rescued by Doctor", built from the same Babel-cited sentence, and shows as "learned in Prologue" because labels are gated on the first ticked story both ends share. Seen in the browser at Episode 06 while checking this version. The fix is the same attribution applied to the label's evidence: feed the model only sentences whose story is ticked, and name that story as where the label was learned.
